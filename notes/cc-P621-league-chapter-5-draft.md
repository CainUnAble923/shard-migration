# League Chapter 5: What We Carry Forward

**Draft for Chase to edit. P621, October 25, 2026.**

Mythril Citizen trial. Blade piece: **the Point**. Place: **a private Exodus trial in Ilshenar**. Chapter question: **Mend it, or sunder it?**

This is a playable story proposal based on [the League storyline](../docs/league-storyline.md), with the voice and continuity of [Chapter 2](cc-P593-league-chapter-2-draft.md), [Chapter 3](cc-P601-league-chapter-3-draft.md), and [Chapter 4](cc-P616-league-chapter-4-draft.md). New dialogue, scenes, encounters, timing, and reward handling are drafts for Chase to edit. They are not implemented features or approved canon. Proposed length: about 40 to 50 minutes, solo or with a party, plus whatever time the player wants for the choice. Exact placement, difficulty, effects, and trial support need implementation review and a client walkthrough.

## What the player takes away

Exodus has held the Point since the Gem broke. Mondain's heir has been trying to put the sword back together and make himself the new keeper of the Gem's power. He has had the last piece. The player has brought the others within reach.

Defeating him leaves a decision that another victory cannot answer. The player can mend the blade and accept responsibility for a gentler loop, or sunder it so that the loop continues without a master. Neither choice brings back everyone who was lost. Neither choice ends Britannia.

Mend makes this character a Keeper. Sunder makes this character a Breaker. The choice is permanent for that character, survives resets, and carries the decided lasting effect, title, and cosmetic. The Ring, the Setting, its two routes, and the Unbroken epilogue belong later. This chapter finishes the search for the blade and gives the player responsibility for what follows.

## Setting and cast

The Exodus trial starts with a sound like a smith testing a hammer on cold metal. The interval never changes. Farther in, the machinery answers the pieces the player carries. A door opens before anyone touches it.

Use a short approach through Exodus Dungeon, then private trial space based on the existing Ver Lor Reg battleground for the final encounter. An inspection room and a forge chamber are staging proposals, not verified rooms or coordinates. The memory scene below is a story reconstruction of the original breaking, not a claim that Mondain's Gem physically stood in this dungeon.

Stock machinery, metal tables, human NPCs, the existing Clockwork Exodus appearance, a sword graphic, a cloak, and available effects should carry the chapter. No new art is required by this draft. Exact cosmetic hues can be chosen from existing hues during implementation.

**Elara Voss, the League Registrar.** She comes to record the recovery of the last piece. She has brought the old expedition reports and a fresh register. Her preference is Mend. She says so, then leaves the decision with the person who carried the pieces back.

**Calder Rusk, the former League champion.** This follows Chapter 4's proposed outcome in which he survives and leaves with Nell. He has agreed to a truce for the approach to Exodus. His preference remains Sunder, but he must answer the difference between ending the world and breaking anyone's claim to own its return. If Chase changes his survival in Chapter 4, this part needs another Unbound speaker.

**Exodus, Mondain's heir.** The existing clockwork body supplies his presence. His proposed dialogue is brief and certain. He sees the repeated losses and offers an owner who can put them to use. He has kept careful records of every failed attempt to join the Point to an incomplete blade.

**The Point.** Black metal held in the forge's central clamp. Its broken end faces an empty channel shaped for the rest of the blade. The very tip is bright from repeated testing. It has never been allowed to cool completely.

Elara and Calder remain in safe scene positions during fights. Neither is a healer, escort target, or required second pair of hands. Their disagreement gives the player people to question without turning either into a menu that speaks only for a reward bonus.

## Beat 1: The last empty space

Offer this as the Mythril chapter after Chapter 4. Characters already at or beyond Mythril can return for the story and rewards under the existing waiver decision. This does not remove a rank already earned or replace the separate promotion job.

At the field office, the four recovered pieces lie on a cloth. Elara has drawn their outlines in the register. The blank space beyond the Blade is small.

**Elara:** "The last piece has answered. Exodus, in Ilshenar."

**Player:** "You sound certain."

**Elara:** "Every time I move the map, they point to the same place. I was hoping they would disagree once."

Calder waits by the door. The damaged League account letter from Chapter 4 lies beside the reports.

**Player:** "You invited him?"

**Elara:** "I asked him to hear what we find before he tries to break it. He asked the same of us."

**Calder:** "I will keep my hands off the pieces. You will let me speak before they are joined."

**Elara:** "Yes."

Optional question, **"What do we know about Exodus?"**

**Elara:** "Mondain's heir. If anyone has kept an account of the Gem breaking, I expect him to have it. I do not expect him to give it freely."

The player can review the earlier reports before accepting. The short recap names Tamsin's return, Mara's company, the feeding, and the empty street. It offers no new reward or decision.

**Player:** "And if there is a way to put the sword together?"

**Elara:** "Then we look at what it would hold together. I promised you that much."

Accept text:

> Enter the Exodus trial. Find the Point and learn what Exodus intends for the whole blade. You may bring your party. The permanent choice comes after the encounter, with a separate confirmation.

A League trial marker at the real Exodus Dungeon entrance offers entry to the private trial. Story access does not require collecting the public encounter's ritual equipment or winning a public boss race. This is proposed access for the chapter only. The public ritual, battleground, rewards, and controller keep their own behavior.

## Beat 2: A place already prepared

Beyond the first door, a short encounter with trial-only mechanical guards clears the approach. Use Exodus creature themes, with the actual roster and Mythril solo balance chosen later.

In the inspection room, the player finds a metal table with four empty hollows. Each matches a piece already recovered. A fifth hollow holds an impression of the Point.

Inspection:

> The edges have been filed and measured again. Someone has spent a long time preparing for pieces that were elsewhere.

A maintenance record is readable beside the table. Its plain words matter more than its age.

> Point retained from the breaking.
>
> Joining attempt: failed. Missing sections cannot carry the load.
>
> Return observed. Power increased.
>
> Joining attempt: failed. Await the remaining sections.

The last two entries repeat down the page. A later line has been added across them:

> Incoming sections detected. Open the way.

**Calder:** "He knew someone would gather them."

**Elara:** "He left us the work."

Selecting **"Compare the pieces with the table"** shows the match without surrendering any keepsake. Recorded recovery is enough if a loose item has been lost.

Exodus speaks through the room's machinery. His physical body remains farther ahead.

**Exodus:** "All four. Set them down."

**Player:** "You have the Point."

**Exodus:** "Since the Gem broke. I have kept what was left to me."

**Player:** "And waited for us."

**Exodus:** "Others began. You finished."

The far door opens. The hammer sound stops for the first time.

Journal update:

> Exodus has kept the Point since the breaking. He has been waiting for the rest of the blade. Enter the forge and examine the joining frame.

## Beat 3: Where it began

The Point hangs in the central clamp. Clockwork Exodus stands behind it. Around the frame, small grooves have been worn into the metal by parts that never quite fit.

**Exodus:** "The Gem held life. The blade took its power when it broke. Each return adds to what it can bear."

**Player:** "We saw what that cost in Doom."

**Exodus:** "I recorded it."

**Calder:** "They were people."

**Exodus:** "They returned."

Select **"Examine the Point"** from a safe position. All five pieces answer. The room falls quiet, and a brief memory surrounds the party.

> A hand closes around the unbroken grip. The blade meets the Gem. For one instant, there is a whole sword and a whole world.
>
> Then the metal breaks.

Keep the Stranger as a figure without a new name, face, or invented speech. The player sees the five breaks along the same sword they have spent the chapters recovering. The Point remains in view when the other pieces scatter. The first return begins before the sound of the blow has finished.

This is the chapter's moment of standing where the loop began, witnessed through the reunited pieces. The party does not travel into an editable past, rescue Mondain, or change the original strike. Do not show the Ring as a recoverable object or reveal what waits beyond the Setting.

As the forge returns, a line of light runs from the clamp into Exodus's body. Another runs outward toward the places the four pieces would occupy.

**Player:** "You are joining yourself to it."

**Exodus:** "It needs a keeper."

**Player:** "One who decides who comes back."

**Exodus:** "One who decides what their return is for."

Inspection of the frame makes the distinction readable:

> The outer supports hold the pieces in alignment. A separate lead binds the completed blade to the clockwork body. That lead was added later.

Elara looks at the added lead before speaking.

**Elara:** "Holding the blade together does not require giving it to him."

**Calder:** "And that lead can be broken without taking the world with it."

The available actions will be demonstrated after Exodus is stopped. This discovery offers two ways forward, not a surprise third ending or a promise that either path costs nothing.

## Beat 4: His terms

Exodus closes the outer doors. Safe retreat to the trial entrance remains available through the chapter interface, even while the fiction holds the room shut.

**Exodus:** "Leave the pieces. You may keep the memory of bringing them."

**Player:** "Tamsin carried one for centuries. You would have left her there."

**Exodus:** "She held it safely."

**Player:** "Mara's company had already won."

**Exodus:** "They could win again."

**Player:** "That is what you mean by keeping us."

Exodus reaches for the joining frame. Elara closes the register and moves clear with Calder.

Ready prompt:

> Defeat Exodus and release the Point from his control. This fight does not choose Mend or Sunder. Begin when your party is ready.

There is no dialogue option to hand him the sword and accidentally end the chapter. His terms establish the threat; the player's personal choice follows his defeat.

## Beat 5: The hand on the frame

Fight proposal: a story version of the Exodus encounter, using the existing clockwork appearance and its energy and vortex themes. Combat numbers, ability frequency, pet handling, and party scaling need a later build spec. Do not assume the existing boss is soloable because the chapter permits solo play.

Give the fight a visible energy sweep with a clear safe area, and brief vortex hazards that can be avoided before they close. Important warnings need text as well as effects. There is room to move without leaving the encounter or standing on a required interaction.

At the first clear stage, Exodus pulls power from the joining frame. The broken edges of the trial's displayed pieces begin to line up. A marked lead becomes usable while his attacks pause.

> The frame is feeding Exodus. Release the marked lead.

Any character can use it. No Tinkering, Magery, faction, or specialist tool is required. The pieces fall out of alignment and the fight resumes.

**Exodus:** "You will need the frame intact."

**Player:** "Then take your hand off it."

At the second stage, the frame begins an automatic restart. The room briefly shows the hammer in its opening position. The journal and a marked control identify the action immediately.

> The frame is trying to begin again. Open the return circuit.

Using the control stops the attempted restart. This is an encounter action, not a character reset or a required death. Completed damage and objectives remain. Ignoring the control leaves a safe, readable interaction stage instead of secretly restoring the boss to full health.

**Exodus:** "I have time."

**Calder, from safety:** "You have had ours."

The final exchange leaves the clockwork body still beside the frame. Its added lead burns dark. The Point remains in its clamp, within reach.

Keep the victory local to this trial. It ends this attempt to claim the blade. It does not declare Exodus permanently dead throughout Britannia, stop public respawns, or replace the ordinary encounter's controller. No boss loot timer should hurry the coming conversation.

Objective:

> Recover the Point. Examine what remains of the frame before deciding the blade's fate.

## Beat 6: The last piece in your hand

Select **"Recover the Point"** to record personal recovery for each eligible participant. The clamp opens without consuming the earlier pieces.

Inspection:

> It is smaller than you expected. The tip has carried the mark of one blow through every return. Near the Blade, the broken edges meet without a gap.

Elara lays the cloth on a safe table. The five pieces fit along it. There is still a visible break between each section.

**Elara:** "The Pommel. The Grip. The Crossguard. The Blade. The Point."

**Player:** "We have it all."

**Elara:** "All the sword. Now we have to decide what to carry out."

Two inspections explain the alternatives before any permanent action appears.

**Inspect the outer supports.** The frame can join the blade while its lead to Exodus stays severed. A small, repeatable motion of light becomes steady. The written explanation says the loop continues in a gentler form, with the character accepting its keeping. No machine names the player ruler of Britannia or grants authority over other characters.

**Inspect the open return circuit.** The remaining joins can be broken so that no keeper can close a hand around the whole blade. The light continues through separate paths. The written explanation says the loop persists without a master; the character carries more of their learned skills through its returns.

These are proposed story demonstrations supported by explicit journal explanations. Neither should depend on recognizing a particular color or interpreting a particle effect.

The player can inspect both, speak to both witnesses, review the reports, or leave with the decision pending. Inspection, dialogue, Point recovery, and approaching the frame never commit a path.

## Beat 7: Two people who came back

Elara opens the reports. Calder stays on the other side of the table. Neither asks the player to hurry.

Question for Elara, **"Why mend it?"**

**Elara:** "Because there are people who will need another morning. I have met them. I would keep that chance and make the return easier to bear."

**Player:** "Exodus said it needed a keeper."

**Elara:** "He meant someone who owned what came back. If we mend it, that lead stays cut. Keeping something alive does not make it yours."

**Player:** "And if we fail them again?"

**Elara:** "Then we answer for it. I cannot offer you a way to care for people that removes that part."

Question for Calder, **"Why sunder it?"**

**Calder:** "Because nobody should be able to close that hand again. Let people keep more of what they learned. Let the return belong to the ones who have to make it."

**Player:** "In the ice, you were willing to end their world."

**Calder:** "Yes. I saw the empty houses. I still raised the frame. You stopped me."

**Player:** "This leaves the world here."

**Calder:** "And leaves no one holding the whole blade. I would take that. I should have waited long enough to find it."

This is a proposed change in what Calder is willing to do, earned by the new evidence. He does not claim he intended this safer outcome in Chapter 4. His admission leaves the earlier conflict intact.

Question for either, **"What happens to the people we brought out?"**

**Elara:** "Tamsin came out. Mara's company finished. We wrote their names. Neither choice takes that away."

**Calder:** "You do not have to put them back to prove you believe in a path."

Optional final question, **"Which would you choose?"**

**Elara:** "Mend."

**Calder:** "Sunder."

Elara turns the register toward the player. The line for the decision is blank.

**Elara:** "You have heard us. Read what each will mean for you. Then put your own name to it."

## Beat 8: A choice with your name on it

Offer a clear comparison before selection. The eventual interface should show the character's name, current loop, and the actual configured effects that apply now and on the next reset. Do not put stale balance numbers into fixed dialogue.

| Path | What the character accepts | Lasting effect from the design | Title and cosmetic |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mend** | Keep the loop in a gentler form, with Exodus's claim severed. The Registrar's people trust the character with its keeping. | A stronger echo for skills below their previous peak, growing with loop count. Level XP uses the same base echo as the other path. | **Keeper**, with an existing cloak in a Keeper hue. |
| **Sunder** | Break the claim to mastery. The loop remains, with no one holding it. The Unbound recognize the character as one of theirs. | Chosen skills retain a floor through resets. More skills and a higher floor are kept as loops advance. Skills not yet kept use base echo. | **Breaker**, with a cracked-steel hue on an existing cloak. |

Both paths keep current-level resets and normal level progression. Neither immediately grants a new skill peak, combat rank, or level. The effects grow by the character's current loop, so someone making the choice late receives the appropriate stage rather than starting the benefit ladder over. The intended end state is parity: Breakers eventually retain every skill at its peak, while Keepers regain skills very quickly.

Numerical basis for the future build: F-12's decided starting shape gives Keeper skill echo from x1.5 at loop 1 to x50 at loop 21, never below base echo. Breaker adds three kept skills per loop past the first, reaching all 58 by loop 21, with the retention floor rising to full peak. The storyline's half-peak starting floor is an overseer default. The exact intervening floor curve, skill selection rules, and balance values need a reviewed implementation spec and playtesting. They are not established by this draft. The older post-cap-loop schedule in F-12 is superseded by its later ladder decision and [the revised levels notes](f12-levels-loops-caps.md).

The story stays at a draft until those effects can be stated accurately in the choice interface. A built version must explain them before commitment rather than promise an unspecified future reward.

Selecting a path opens a second, personal confirmation. Proposed text:

> **Mend the blade: become a Keeper.**
>
> This choice is permanent for this character and survives every reset. The loop continues. Your lasting benefit is stronger skill echo below your previous peaks. Review your current and next-loop values below.
>
> Confirm Mend, or return to the comparison without choosing.

Or:

> **Sunder the blade: become a Breaker.**
>
> This choice is permanent for this character and survives every reset. The loop continues without a master. Your lasting benefit is a growing set of chosen skills that keep a floor through resets. Review the number of skills and retention floor for your current and next loop below.
>
> Confirm Sunder, or return to the comparison without choosing.

Include **"Decide later"** on the comparison. Closing a window, a timeout, leaving the party, another player's click, or losing a connection never supplies consent. No default path is preselected. Starting a preview is reversible; confirming the named path is the permanent action.

## Beat 9A: Mend

After personal confirmation, the player closes the outer supports with Exodus's lead visibly broken. The five sections meet. A fine line remains where the Blade and Point were joined.

> The sword holds. The pull toward another return is still there. This time it does not drag your hand toward the frame.

There is no sudden resurrection of the dead and no rewind of the fight. The scene makes a promise about how the character will live with the next loop, without granting or forcing that reset now.

**Elara:** "Keeper."

**Player:** "Of the loop."

**Elara:** "And answerable to the people in it. I will remember that part when I ask something of you."

Calder checks the broken lead before he looks at the player.

**Calder:** "I would have broken it."

**Player:** "I know."

**Calder:** "Then leave that cut where you can see it."

The proposed Keeper cloak waits beside the register. The player can collect or equip it later. Its hue is a mark of the decision, not a required item for the lasting effect.

## Beat 9B: Sunder

After personal confirmation, the player opens the return circuit and releases the frame's pressure across the remaining joins. The sections separate. The lead to Exodus falls loose with nothing left to fasten to.

> The sword cannot be held whole. The pull toward another return remains, but it has no single hand behind it.

For a moment, ordinary remembered actions pass through the player's hands: tying a bandage, setting a tool, finding the balance of a blade. These are images of learned skill, not free gains or a forced selection of particular skills.

**Calder:** "Breaker."

**Player:** "The world is still here."

**Calder:** "Yes."

He listens to the machinery slowing before adding anything.

**Calder:** "I would like to go back and hear it."

Elara turns the register toward the player.

**Elara:** "I would have mended it. Your name still belongs on this page."

**Player:** "What will you write?"

**Elara:** "What you chose. And that you came back."

The proposed Breaker cloak uses an existing cracked-steel hue. It can be collected or equipped later. Skill choices belong in a separate, clear management step with the actual retention rules shown. Deferring those skill selections cannot change Sunder into Mend, apply arbitrary defaults, or lose the character's earned selection entitlement. Exact selection and replacement rules remain implementation work.

Both paths offer a safe return to the real Exodus Dungeon entrance, then the field office. There is no forced next loop. The character can finish at their own pace.

At the office, the street is occupied. A cart is being unloaded. Someone asks whether the blue cloth has come in, recalling the list in Chapter 4's empty market.

**Player:** "I heard that in the vision. Or saw it written down."

**Elara:** "Then someone still has an errand to finish."

She records the Point's recovery and the character's confirmed path.

**Elara:** "Exodus held the last piece. You kept him from taking the rest."

**Player:** "And chose what to do with it."

**Elara:** "Yes. I will put that in your words."

Keeper report line:

> I mended the blade. I will keep the return, and answer for how I use it.

Breaker report line:

> I sundered the blade. The return remains. No one will hold it whole.

Optional question, **"Are we finished?"**

**Elara:** "With finding the sword, yes. With living here, I hope not."

She closes this report. Outside, someone calls for help with the last crate. The player can leave the office while the ordinary day continues.

Do not recover the Ring, open the Setting, introduce its intruders or bosses, award the Unbroken title, or assemble the final crest here. Chapter 6 has its own discovery to make.

## Reward and completion handling, proposed

- **The Point:** the fifth recovered blade piece, recorded before the decision. Suggested journal text: "Taken from the frame where Exodus waited for the rest of the sword." Personal recovery remains recorded after either path.
- **The blade after the choice:** propose a personal, noncombat display keepsake reflecting the character's path, a mended sword for Keeper or separated sections for Breaker. Use existing sword and item graphics. Preserve inspection of the earlier pieces through the journal; do not let the fiction of joining or sundering erase earned recovery. Exact custody and display handling remain for Chase's edit.
- **Permanent path:** save Mend or Sunder per character, independent of inventory, current level, and loop resets. Its lasting effect belongs to that record. Losing a cloak or display item cannot remove it or open another choice.
- **Title and cosmetic:** Keeper and a Keeper cloak hue, or Breaker and a cracked-steel cloak hue. Use existing assets. No combat stats or new art dependency. Replacement restores the same earned cosmetic entitlement.
- **Chapter completion:** record Chapter 5 after personal confirmation and the report to Elara. Personal Point recovery and boss victory persist while a player decides later. Keep the separate Renown, guild standing, promotion-job, and existing chapter-waiver rules.
- **Other payouts:** leave XP, Renown, and gold amounts to the build prompt. Do not promise public Exodus artifact rolls, stat rewards, or repeatable boss farming from the story trial.

As in the earlier drafts, a full backpack should leave earned items available from Elara. Reward delivery is separate from earning the path or recovery record. Reentry restores the same entitlement, not a second permanent choice or another earned reward.

The decided tally counts each character's confirmed choice once. Its only shared-world effect is the larger side coloring seasonal notices and event spawns. This draft adds no majority vote over an individual's choice, world reset, global dungeon change, or extra combat advantage. Exact seasonal content remains separate work. The Registrar's people trusting Keepers and the Unbound recognizing Breakers can have personal dialogue here without blocking either path from ordinary League services.

## Retry, choice, and party notes for the eventual build

Keep objectives explicit: inspect the prepared table, witness the original breaking, defeat Exodus, recover the Point, examine both outcomes, choose when ready, report to Elara. Required dialogue and explanations can be revisited. A failed fight restarts that encounter without replaying completed investigation within a continuing trial. Reset encounters do not pay farming loot.

Private trial travel, boss state, and exit handling need their own implementation review. The existing ritual explicitly sends players and pets to Ilshenar, and the public controller exposes shared encounter state. A trial cannot assume that copying scenery also isolates those paths. No part of this draft authorizes changing the public encounter.

Party members share investigation and combat, then each eligible character receives a personal choice scene after the common victory. A party leader never decides for another member. Friends can choose different paths in the same run. Their mended or sundered blade scenes and display items are personal outcomes, so one character's confirmation cannot destroy another's unfinished choice or overwrite the shared world.

No majority, synchronized switch, particular guild, or minimum party size is required. Helpers who have already chosen can join the fight without receiving another path or adding another tally entry. Characters earning their own credit follow Chapters 1 through 4 first; friends at other stages can help under the eventual participation rules.

Provide a safe place outside an expiring trial to resume the comparison and personal scene through Elara. Nobody should have to choose quickly to avoid losing a cleared boss. Defeat, trial closure, leaving early, and disconnect before confirmation leave the path unchosen. After confirmation, a disconnect preserves the chosen path, its effect, and its pending rewards. The completion report and item collection can resume later.

The future build must record a confirmed choice once, award the matching entitlement once, and count it once in the tally even if a player retries collection or reconnects during the scene. If a path already exists, show it and resume its aftermath. Never offer the opposite confirmation as a recovery shortcut. Those are proposed persistence requirements, not claims that this choice system already exists.
