# Crafting on Shattered Legacy

Draft checked against project notes and source code through October 22, 2026. Features still to come are marked (planned). Availability and settings on the running shard have not been checked. Configurable numbers below are source defaults.

## Starting a craft

Crafting here adds learned designs, guild work, and materials that support training toward 200 skill. Start with your trade's guildmaster in New Haven. Open the Guild Directory with `[guild` and choose **Show me the way** for your guild.

| Craft | Guild for patterns and bulk orders | Town vendors for bulk orders |
|---|---|---|
| Blacksmithy | Society of Smiths | Blacksmith, Weaponsmith |
| Tailoring | Tailors' Circle | Tailor, Weaver |
| Carpentry | Foresters' Union | Carpenter |
| Bowcraft and Fletching | Foresters' Union | Bowyer |
| Tinkering | Tinkers' Union | Tinker |
| Alchemy | Order of the Alembic | Alchemist |
| Inscription | Arcane Society | Scribe |
| Cooking | Maritime Brotherhood | Cook, Baker |

Joining a guild is free. Guild membership, standing, and currency belong to your character. Learned recipes belong to your account, so another character on the same account can use them once that character meets the crafting requirements.

## Learn a design before making it

In the crafts above, ordinary designs with a minimum crafting skill of **30.0 or less** are known from the start. Higher designs are hidden until learned. A design with its own recipe scroll keeps that separate requirement, even if its minimum skill is low.

Bands use the design's minimum skill, not your current skill or the material you select.

| Design's minimum skill | How it is learned |
|---|---|
| Above 30 through 50 | Band I pattern book |
| Above 50 through 70 | Band II pattern book |
| Above 70 through 90 | Band III pattern book |
| Above 90 through 100 | Band IV pattern book |
| Above 100 through 120 | Band V individual recipe scroll |
| Above 120 through 140 | Band VI individual recipe scroll |
| Above 140 through 160 | Band VII individual recipe scroll |
| Above 160 through 180 | Band VIII individual recipe scroll |
| Above 180 through 200 | Band IX individual recipe scroll |

These are learning bands, not a promise that every craft has designs in every band. A book band with fewer than 5 ordinary designs folds forward into the next book band, possibly as far as IV. Follow the hint in your craft menu and the guild shop's actual book list.

Open **Pattern books and recipe scrolls** at the owning guildmaster. Ordinary book prices are **100, 200, 400, and 800 guild currency** for Bands I through IV. For unmerged bands, guild promotions grant the corresponding books at rank steps 1 through 4, and the shop sells them one rank step earlier. Merged books follow the shop's actual list. Promotions check both standing and base crafting skill. Guilds use their own rank names.

At rank steps 4 and 5, the shop has daily Band V rotations of up to **3 and 5 scrolls** respectively, when designs exist. Rank step 5 also grants up to **2 unknown Band V starter scrolls for each craft taught by the guild**. Top-rank guild trials are (planned), with their requirement waived by default. A rotation scroll costs **1,500 guild currency**, plus the following material where listed.

| Craft | Additional cost per Band V shop scroll |
|---|---|
| Blacksmithy, Tinkering | 10 Platinum Ingots |
| Carpentry, Fletching | 10 Ironwood Boards |
| Tailoring | 10 Barbed Leather |
| Alchemy, Inscription, Cooking | None |

Read a pattern book or account recipe scroll from your backpack. Learning consumes it once; crafting does not consume another copy each time. A copy your account already knows is kept. Unread copies can be traded. Learning does not replace the item's skill, tools, or material requirements.

Existing recipe scrolls and the Masonry and Glassblowing books also share their knowledge across the account. Masonry and Glassblowing retain their whole-craft book requirements. Ordinary spell scrolls use the spell in your spellbook instead of these pattern bands. Basket weaving also needs its own teaching book, in addition to the applicable Tinkering patterns.

Cartography has no pattern bands. Imbuing has its own formula-learning system; see the [Artificers' Order guide](artificers-order.md).

Recipe scrolls can also appear in loot boxes and monster-stealing rewards. A completed large Smith order on the Society payout path has a default **2 percent** chance at a Band V Blacksmithy scroll. Commission recipe rewards and the broader epic crafting system are (planned).

Repairing, smelting, and enhancing do not require the pattern books above.

## Exceptional gear and material bonuses

An exceptional item gets **1.5 times its positive material bonuses, rounded half up**, wherever the material supplies those bonuses through the metal, leather, or scale rules. A material's +5 bonus becomes +8. Negative material penalties stay unchanged. Runic properties and the separate bonuses for exceptional quality are not multiplied by this rule. Normal wearer caps still apply.

Stock woods get the multiplier on their bow bonuses. Extended woods use their own **normal bonus plus exceptional additions** instead; do not multiply those tables again. Cloth has no material bonus for this rule to multiply.

Arms Lore also improves exceptional crafting. Its contribution takes an exceptional weapon's quality damage bonus from **35 at 0 Arms Lore to 50 at 200**, before separate material bonuses. It can add up to **10 extra armor resistance points**. Arms Lore also reduces wear, supports field maintenance of your own damaged gear, and reduces the risk of weakening gear during a tool repair.

## Materials beyond Valorite and Frostwood

Blacksmithy and metal-based Tinkering use the following extended metal ladder. These materials also have matching Mining thresholds for mining ore, smelting ore, and recycling with the Smith Guild Salvage Bag.

| Metal | Base crafting skill needed | Material training ceiling |
|---|---:|---:|
| Platinum | 112.5 | 125.0 |
| Toxic | 125.0 | 137.5 |
| Blaze | 137.5 | 150.0 |
| Frost | 150.0 | 162.5 |
| Obsidian | 162.5 | 175.0 |
| Mythril | 175.0 | 187.5 |
| Adamantium | 187.5 | 200.0 |
| Celestial | 200.0 | 200.0 |

Carpentry and Fletching have a separate wood ladder. These are crafting requirements, not Lumberjacking requirements.

| Wood | Base crafting skill needed | Material training ceiling | Weapon theme |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Ironwood | 100.0 | 115.0 | Durability; exceptional damage and lower stat requirements. |
| Ghostwood | 115.0 | 130.0 | Hit chance; exceptional damage and defense chance. |
| Emberbark | 130.0 | 140.0 | Fire damage; exceptional damage and Hit Fire Area. |
| Frostbark | 140.0 | 155.0 | Cold damage; exceptional damage and Hit Lower Attack. |
| Shadowbark | 155.0 | 170.0 | Swing speed; exceptional damage and hit chance. |
| Runewood | 170.0 | 185.0 | Energy damage and mana regeneration; exceptional damage and mana leech. |
| Voidwood | 185.0 | 200.0 | Life leech; exceptional damage and stamina leech. |
| Starwood | 200.0 | 200.0 | Luck; exceptional damage, hit chance, and a random Heartwood-style extra. |

Extended wood bonuses apply to bows, staves, clubs, and wooden shields made from those woods. Shields have their own defensive bonuses. These woods do not add Self Repair. Rootwood and Dawnstone are separate starter materials, worked at **45.0** in their corresponding crafts, outside the extended training ladders. Rootwood adds no wood combat bonus.

For Blacksmithy, Tinkering, Carpentry, and Fletching, the training ceiling can extend a recipe's gain range when it actually consumes the selected metal or wood. Harder recipes remain better practice. This changes the gain range without changing the recipe's success chance or exceptional chance. Skill caps still apply, and gains are not guaranteed. Celestial and Starwood require 200 already, so they add no training beyond the cap. Cutting logs into boards does not provide this crafting gain path.

## Bulk order deeds across the crafts

Bulk order deeds, or BODs, cover the eight crafts in the first table. Use a vendor's **Bulk Order Info** entry, or the owning guildmaster as a member. Having more than zero base skill is required for the ordinary vendor offer. The Foresters' guildmaster asks which craft when you qualify for both Carpentry and Fletching.

The ordinary reward-point system allows up to **3 accumulated offers per craft**, with one replenished every **6 hours**. This is an offer allowance, not a limit of three carried deeds. Large deeds can appear from **70.1 base skill**. Turn in completed deeds to an appropriate giver; gold goes to your bank and the reward window offers immediate rewards or saved points. Points are separate for each craft.

Banking keeps **2 percent of a small deed's points or 20 percent of a large deed's points** for later rewards. The deed window lets you choose whether to be asked, claim immediately, or bank automatically. Settle pending rewards before another ordinary turn-in. Some catalog entries say **not on the shard yet** and cannot be bought.

Small deeds consume matching items. Large deeds normally consume the matching filled small deeds. **Large Smith deeds also accept matching crafted items directly**, while retaining the small-deed route. Deed windows offer **combine with contained items** for filling from a container. Match the deed's quantity, material, and exceptional requirement. Split an oversized stack to the amount still needed.

The Society of Smiths has its own member system: **3 small and 1 large deed at a time**, with no offer cooldown at the Society. Its book and guildmaster let you choose **Orders that still teach me**, bank Smithing Seals, or cash out. Smith turn-ins give standing and Blacksmithy checks, and Smith deeds require crafted gear. A member's turn-in at a regular smith follows the Society payout setting too. See the [Society of Smiths guide](society-of-smiths.md) for those rules.

Moving the other crafts into guild currencies and the same teaching-order rules is (planned). Larger rank-based deed allowances are (planned). Cartography and Fishing deeds are (planned); gathering and taming use their separate guild contracts.

## Commissions, contracts, and requisitions

| Work | What it asks for | Current reward route |
|---|---|---|
| BOD | Quantities of named crafted goods. | Craft reward points, or the Society's Smithing Seal system. |
| Guild Contract | A listed supply or delivery job. | That guild's standing and currency, plus listed gold. |
| Smith commission | A named client's piece or matching set, including material and quality. | Smithing Seals, Society standing, and a Blacksmithy check. |
| Requisition | A matching magic item, found or crafted, sometimes with a slayer requirement. | Renown, the requesting guild's standing, and the Appraiser's gold price. |

Current Smith commissions are on the Society guildmaster's **Commissions** page and in the Smithing Guild Book. You may hold **3 small and 1 large commission**, separately from deeds and contracts. Sets accept pieces one at a time and pay when complete. There is no completion deadline. Abandoning a commission starts a default **5-minute wait** before another small request.

Moving client commissions to the League, adding Tailoring, recurring clients, Renown, deadlines, and Infamy penalties are (planned). Player-posted Citizen Commissions are also (planned).

Requisitions are already built. Open the League's **Requisitions board** or a guild's **Requisitions** page. Each character normally gets **3 requests per posting guild**, refreshed daily. Guild rank sets the quality and pay, limited by League rank. A request with a live Lead stays through the daily refresh. Turn in a matching item from your backpack; it is consumed. Unfilled requests expire quietly, without a Renown penalty or Infamy. See the [League guide](league.md) for Leads and the Appraiser.

## Levelable crafted gear

The item-level engine is built, but its **Smith's Seal** has no normal player source yet. Player access is (planned); this is not an available reward to look for in the Society catalog. The seal item is separate from the Smithing Seals currency.

The built rules allow crafted weapons, armor, shields, and jewelry to become levelable. Found gear, artifacts, and clothing are ineligible. Once activated, worn gear earns XP when a creature dies, based on your share of damage. Pet, summon, and spell damage can count for the owner. Every equipped levelable item receives the owner's share, subject to a per-item limit; gear in the backpack earns none.

The default maximum is **item level 200**. Each level gives **4 points**, with **8 instead on every tenth level**. Points buy supported properties and resistances within item-kind, property-slot, and property-value limits. They do not buy skill bonuses, Self Repair, or weapon hit effects. Earlier pacing estimates are not a promise of leveling time.

## Salvage and resmelting

Recycling consumes the item. Keep anything you want to save outside the bag you are salvaging.

The Blacksmithy menu's **Smelt** action recycles eligible metal gear, returning half the recipe's ingot cost for crafted pieces, rounded down. Noncrafted gear returns 1 ingot. This is separate from ore smelting and from a salvage bag's return formula.

The Society gives members a blessed **Smith Guild Salvage Bag** when they join. Put eligible metal gear inside and choose **Salvage Ingots** near a forge, with a smithing tool in your backpack. This bag supports the extended metals and checks Mining for them. Higher Mining improves crafted-item returns, with a limit below the recipe cost for ordinary recipes. A crafted Plate Chest costing 25 ingots returns **17 at 100 Mining**. Noncrafted eligible gear returns **2 ingots**. Successful metal salvage also awards Society members Smithing Seals by material.

With scissors in your backpack, **Salvage Cloth** cuts eligible cloth and leather items. **Salvage All** handles both categories when offered. Repair, enhancement, cloth cutting, ore smelting, and salvaging gear are distinct actions; do not expect them to share costs or return amounts.

**Make X** can count finished items or attempts. In items mode, **Exceptional only** keeps working toward the requested exceptional pieces and recycles or routes ordinary results. The defaults cap a run at **100 finished items or 500 attempts** in the respective modes. Attempts use the normal crafting delay and materials. Moving, running out of materials, a worn-out tool, or pressing **Stop** ends the run.

Automatic reject handling uses an ordinary Salvage Bag when one is present and suitable; at the end it salvages that bag's contents. It can otherwise smelt Smith rejects, cut Tailoring rejects with scissors, or route them to your trash or chosen rejects bag. The Smith Guild Salvage Bag is a separate bag type; do not assume it is the ordinary bag used by this automation.

## Contradictions

These differences remain on record without deciding between the sources.

- **BOD rewards and teaching:** F-18, F-20, and F-25 decide guild currencies and broader teaching-order rules. P122, P123, and current source use ordinary reward points for the other crafts while preserving the separate Society system. F-18's one turn-in check per five items also differs from the current Smith per-deed checks.
- **Deed allowances:** F-25 retains larger guild-rank and League-rank allowances. Current Society code still fixes its carried limit at 3 small and 1 large deed.
- **Recipe inventory and rewards:** P78 reports no Smith Band IV book and no loot-box caller. Current Exodus crafting adds a Band IV dagger, and current loot boxes and monster stealing call recipe reward tables. F-43 describes a large-BOD scroll chance generally; the present payout hook is Smith-only. Its commission recipe selection is empty by default.
- **Merged-book ranks:** P78 describes a folded Band IV book as sold at rank step 3 and granted at step 4. Current shop and promotion code merge the original band's book after choosing its rank step, while menu hints name the final band's rank. The displayed hint and actual book access can differ.
- **Exceptional woods:** F-29's suggested wording combines a 1.5 material multiplier with exceptional-only additions. P228 and current source leave P227's extended wood tables outside that multiplier, using their separate exceptional additions.
- **Wood history and harvesting:** F-28 assumes an earlier Rootwood bow-bonus hook; P36 and P227 record Rootwood as cosmetic. Crafting now has the stepped wood ladder above, while Lumberjacking still has separate harvest requirements and gain ranges. They are not interchangeable tables.
- **Commissions:** F-37 places commissions at the League with Renown, deadlines, and penalties. Current Smith commissions remain at the Society, with Seals and standing and no completion deadline.
- **Levelable gear:** P72 proposes a different XP curve, one point per level, and a material point budget. P261 and current code use the rules above without that material budget. The acquisition method for the Smith's Seal remains undecided.
- **Requisition refresh:** F-47 describes daily rollover of open requests. P150 and current source preserve a request while its Lead remains live.
- **Salvage automation:** the Smith Guild Salvage Bag describes itself as replacing the ordinary bag for members. Current Make X reject handling specifically looks for the ordinary Salvage Bag; the two bag types are separate.
