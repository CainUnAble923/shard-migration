# Levels, loops, power scrolls, and your New Haven start

Draft checked against project notes and source code on October 21, 2026. Features still to come are marked (planned). Availability and settings on the running shard have not been checked. Configurable behavior below describes the source defaults.

## Start with the recruiter

Your first tutorial starts in New Haven, Trammel. Look for **Rook Hallowell, the League recruiter**, near the arrival point in the plaza. Double-click him to open the tutorial page. He is separate from Elara Voss, the League Registrar.

The required core covers movement, your paperdoll, your backpack, chat and the journal, death and resurrection, banking, travel, the League, and a loop vision. Finish the core, then return to Rook to graduate. The extra training wings are optional.

The tutorial framework is built. Many station lessons still show an explanation instead of a playable trial. More hands-on trials are (planned).

The loop vision is a demonstration. It changes none of your skills, stats, or loop progress.

**Graduation is your first real loop.** Your character starts at loop 0. Graduation records loop 1, gives its loop reward, and permits leaving Haven island under the default travel rules. You can visit the island's tutorial dungeon before graduating.

Graduation also gives an Explorer's tier 1 loot box and offers a starter evolution pet. If you already chose a pet at the tutorial farm, graduation does not offer another. Open your graduation box in town as the tutorial suggests.

The tutorial is once per account, but loop progress belongs to each character. Later characters on a graduated account receive their own first loop when they log in, without repeating the normal tutorial rewards.

Rook also offers a skip with two confirmations. Under the current provisional default, skipping records the first loop and opens island travel. If that option is disabled, you must return for remedial training and graduate before leaving. A skipper can return to Rook for remedial training; finishing it gives the normal graduation rewards without recording the first loop twice.

## What works now

Recording the first loop is built. The full player level and XP system is (planned). Graduation currently keeps your skills and stats; it does not perform the future level or skill reset.

The current skill policy defaults to a flat **200.0 cap per skill**, independent of loop count. The planned 50 and 100 level caps below do not currently restrict your skills. A cap is permission to train higher, not a promise that every activity teaches all the way to it. Some high-skill training content is still (planned).

Creature comparisons and loot boxes currently estimate player strength from skills in different ways. Those estimates are not the planned XP level and should not be used to track progress toward a loop.

## Three numbers to know (planned)

| Number | What it means in the planned system |
|---|---|
| Current level | Your progress within this loop. Every real loop returns it to 1, before any saved XP is credited. |
| Maximum level | Your permanent ceiling. Loops raise it along the ladder below. Each individual skill's training cap will follow this number, rather than your current level. |
| Loop count | How many loops you have completed. It is retained through later loops. The first loop is already recorded at tutorial graduation. |

Skills remain earned by use. Levels and loops will not hand out free skill or stat points. The design allows one character to train every skill, rather than requiring you to divide a small total skill allowance among trades.

## The 21-loop ladder (planned)

Creation is loop 0, with a maximum level of 50. Graduation is loop 1, with a maximum level of 100. Each later loop raises the maximum by 5, reaching 200 at loop 21.

## What a loop resets and keeps (planned)

Later loops are chosen at the current cap, with confirmation. They are required to raise the maximum, but reaching the cap does not automatically reset you.

| Progress | Through a planned loop |
|---|---|
| Current level | Returns to 1. |
| Maximum level and completed loops | Retained; the next ladder step raises the maximum. |
| Skills | Reset or retained according to the Keeper and Breaker paths below. |
| Stats | Kept. |
| Achievements, AP, Renown, titles, collections, and learned recipes | Kept. |
| League progress, guild membership, and guild ranks | Kept. |
| Evolution pet stage | Kept. |
| Pet and stable capacity | Intended to follow permanent progress, so a level reset does not shrink it. |
| Gear | Kept, but custom gear above your current level becomes usable again as you climb. The decided newbie dungeon tier 1 set has no level requirement. Stock UO gear receives no new level requirement. |

The planned **echo**, or loop memory, speeds up progress below your previous peaks. New ground uses the normal base rate.

The **Keeper** path rebuilds skills with a stronger skill echo. The **Breaker** path retains more chosen skills through successive loops. Both still reset current level and use the same level XP echo. These paths and their reset behavior are (planned).

## Power scrolls (planned)

In the loop design, a power scroll is the key to the next maximum level. The first loop needs no scroll. Every step above 100 needs the next target scroll and a loop together, through 200. Reading a scroll alone will not provide a separate cap increase.

The decided sources include paragons, champion spawns, and bosses, with the New Haven dungeon's mini champion intended as the first source (planned). The League will combine lower scrolls into higher ones for Renown, with League rank requirements (planned). This guide gives no drop rates, merge costs, or waiting-time estimates.

**Those loop scrolls are not implemented yet.** Existing stock power scrolls still check and raise a named skill's cap. Under the default flat 200 skill policy, a stock 105, 110, 115, or 120 scroll is refused because the skill cap is already higher. It does not unlock loop 2.

## XP at the cap (planned)

The decided XP sources are activity: kills, crafting, harvesting, new skill peaks, and discoveries. Currency turn-ins and achievements do not separately pay level XP.

At your maximum level, eligible XP will fill a reserve sized as XP for **five levels**. After a loop, that reserve is credited as raw XP starting at level 1, without an echo multiplier on the credit. It is an amount of XP, not a promise to start exactly five levels higher. The exact reserve valuation and reset transaction remain proposals in P282.

Once the reserve is full, extra XP is lost. The design has no reserve at the final maximum of 200. These rules and the XP curve are (planned); published pacing calculations are estimates, not measured play times.

## Young status

Young lasts **336 hours of account play time**, or two weeks spent playing. It does not expire two calendar weeks after account creation. The login message shows the remaining play time.

Raising skills does not end Young. Graduation, the loop vision, and later recorded loops do not end it either. Graduating opens island travel while your Young clock continues.

Young belongs to the account. When it ends, it ends for every character on that account. A murder count still ends it early. To give it up voluntarily, say **"I want to grow up!"** and confirm. The standard **"I renounce my young player status"** phrase also works.

Young protection has exceptions, including tutorial fights. It does not make every creature harmless.

## Contradictions

These differences remain on record without deciding between the sources.

- **Ladder:** older queue passages retain a maximum of 1,000 and large jumps. Older scroll notes describe +25 steps. The queue's later ladder, P282, and P371 describe 50, 100, then +5 through 200 at loop 21.
- **Design and current skills:** the design ties each skill cap to maximum level. Current code applies a flat default 200 cap and leaves the level and skill reset hook empty, even when graduation records loop 1.
- **Skill retention:** the vision says your skills reset with your level. The later Keeper and Breaker decisions describe different skill-retention rules, with Breakers eventually keeping every skill.
- **First-loop timing:** older F-12 modeling treats the first loop as an at-cap reset. P371 makes tutorial graduation the first loop; current graduation checks core completion rather than a player level of 50.
- **Reserve:** the queue retains a 25-percent reserve. P282 records approval for XP for five levels. Its exact valuation and transaction details remain proposed.
- **Post-cap progress:** older queue paragraphs put another 20 progression loops after reaching 200. The later ladder places the scroll progression before 200 and the capstone at loop 21.
- **Young:** P371 records the change from first-loop Young removal to keeping Young through loops. The retained murder and voluntary-renunciation rules are also exceptions to the phrase "time only."
- **Tutorial:** F-35 describes a continuous story questline with hands-on steps. P80's framework and current code use core lessons and optional wings, with explanatory placeholders for many trials. P276 records a built farm lesson but leaves world placement and client verification unfinished.
- **Displayed strength:** creature comparisons use the best combat skill by default; loot boxes use total base skills divided by 10. Neither is the planned XP level, and the two estimates can differ.
- **Scrolls:** the design uses scrolls for level-cap loops. Current stock scrolls still address individual skills. The old Smith catalog scroll sales described in P20 were removed in P23.
