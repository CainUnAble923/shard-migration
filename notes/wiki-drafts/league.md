# League of Extraordinary Citizens

Draft from project notes through October 21, 2026. Features still to come are marked (planned). Availability on the running shard has not been checked.

## What the League is

The League of Extraordinary Citizens, or LXC, is "the guild for the guilds." It connects Britannia's guilds and gives citizens a place to find work, build a reputation, and claim rewards. It is open from day one.

Start with Elara Voss, the League Registrar, at the field office in New Haven. Open the Guild Directory with `[guild` and use the League row at the top. **Show me the way** points you toward her.

Registering starts you as an Iron Citizen. League registration belongs to your account, while each character has their own League rank. Your standing in individual guilds is separate from that rank.

Three account-wide numbers serve different purposes:

| Number | What it does |
|---|---|
| Achievement Points, or AP | A permanent achievement score that opens reward-shop tiers and titles. You do not spend it. |
| Renown | The balance you spend in the League shop. Achievements and requisition turn-ins earn it. |
| Lifetime Renown | The earned total used for promotion requirements. Shop purchases leave it unchanged. |

At the Registrar, open **League Rank** to see the requirements for your next promotion. You need enough Lifetime Renown and the required ranks in guilds you have joined. Higher League ranks ask for standing in more guilds. The published Renown thresholds are placeholders, so use the Registrar's displayed requirements.

A promotion job at each rank and story trials at milestone ranks are part of the design (planned). Unbuilt jobs and chapters are waived by default. A chapter added later does not take away a rank you already earned or become a requirement for a promotion you already passed.

The milestone metals are Dull Copper, Bronze, Verite, Blaze, Mythril, and Celestial. Each milestone adds 100 bank item slots, taking the base 1,000 slots to 1,600 at Celestial. Extra League job slots and bulk order deed allowances at milestones are still to come (planned).

## What the League hands out

### Guild quests and adventuring work (planned)

The broader League job board is designed to offer work you can do alone or share with your party. Boards are planned for towns, taverns, and dungeon entrances.

| Work (planned) | What you will do |
|---|---|
| Guild quests | Gather, craft, or deliver goods for a guild. Some jobs serve several guilds. |
| Monster hunts | Hunt a named or elite target for monster parts. |
| Monster culling | Kill a requested number of a species in a particular region. |
| Dungeon dives | Gather resources, cull creatures, or investigate something that brings out a temporary mini boss. |

The decided rewards include Renown, XP, gold, and the paying guild's currency and reputation. Multi-guild jobs pay the full reward for each participating guild. For these planned jobs, rewards owed by a guild you have not joined wait until you join and finish its Apprentice task.

The decided League job limit starts at 3 and gains one slot per milestone, reaching 9. Guildmaster ledgers keep their separate limits. These limits describe the planned League jobs, not the requisitions below.

### Commissions (planned)

The notes distinguish two kinds of commission:

| Kind (planned) | Who asks, and what it means |
|---|---|
| Client Commissions, a proposed name | An NPC client wants a specific crafted piece by a deadline. The existing Smith commissions are to move to the League, with Tailoring next. Play-earned Renown will count toward League promotion. |
| Citizen Commissions | A player posts a request and puts the reward aside in advance. Renown paid by another player will be spendable, but will not add to Lifetime Renown or buy League rank. |

For Client Commissions, the approved starting deadline is 3 days. Expiry or abandonment is planned to cost 10 percent of the reward's Renown and add Infamy. Infamy is still planned. These penalties do not apply to requisitions or Leads.

### Requisitions and Leads

Requisitions give found or crafted magic items a destination. Open **Requisitions board** at the Registrar, use the field-office board, or open a guild's **Requisitions** page. The Appraiser can also show you which request wants an item.

Each character gets 3 requests per posting guild, refreshed daily. The Custodians do not post requests. Guild rank sets the quality and pay, while League rank limits the tier you can receive. Requests follow each guild's interests, such as bows for the Rangers or spellbooks and jewelry for the Arcane Society. Slayer weapons can be requested too.

Turn in a matching item from your backpack at an appropriate board or clerk. The item is consumed, and you receive Renown, that guild's standing, and gold equal to the Appraiser's price. Requisition Renown counts toward promotion. Non-members can fill the lowest-tier requests; the build notes record their standing being paid immediately.

An unfilled request normally rolls over without a Renown penalty or Infamy. A filled slot waits until the next daily refresh. Unrequested magic items can instead be sold to the Appraiser for a modest gold payment.

A request may include a **Lead**, a personal rumor naming an area and creature group. Qualifying kills there give you an extra chance at an item that fills the request. The chance increases until a drop is guaranteed on the 40th qualifying kill under the recorded defaults. The item goes into the corpse, so remember to loot it.

Leads last 3 days and expire quietly. A request with a live Lead stays through the daily refresh. Only your chances change. The kill must match the Lead's area and creature group; pets, summons, and creatures below the default 1,000 Fame minimum do not count. Better Leads become available as League rank rises.

## The reward shop

**AP unlocks, Renown buys.** Open **League Shop** at the Registrar or from the `[achievements` window. Locked rows show the AP you need. A purchase asks for confirmation and never reduces Lifetime Renown or League rank. League registration is not required to shop.

The recorded starter prices are:

| AP needed | Reward | Renown |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Registered Adventurer title | 25 |
| 0 | League tabard | 50 |
| 0 | System's Bronze Loot Box | 40 |
| 100 | Hair restyling deed | 75 |
| 100 | Blessed rune marked to the New Haven Registrar | 60 |
| 250 | League banner | 150 |
| 250 | League cloak | 200 |
| 250 | System's Silver Loot Box | 150 |
| 500 | Trophy statuette showing the buyer's name and AP | 250 |
| 500 | League-hue pigment, one use | 300 |
| 500 | Veteran of the League title | 200 |
| 1,000 | System's Gold Loot Box | 400 |
| 1,750 | League regalia: robe, cloak, and sandals | 1,000 |
| 1,750 | The System's Favorite title | 500 |

The shop sells cosmetics, decor, and conveniences. It does not sell combat properties, power scrolls, artifacts, or skill and stat increases. The tabard cannot be dyed.

Bronze, Silver, and Gold boxes contain 1, 2, and 3 draws respectively from League goods allowed by the box's AP tier. They do not pay gold or Renown. Boxes are bound to your account and open in a guarded town or a house where you have access as an owner, co-owner, or friend.

AP also opens free titles: the Noted at 100, the Accomplished at 250, the Distinguished at 500, the Renowned at 1,000, and the Illustrious at 1,750. Open **Titles** to choose one. Owned titles belong to the account, and each character chooses which one to display after their name.

The shop's **Hall** page adds display cases, dragon and gargoyle statues, wall trophies, and mounted heads. AP opens the rows and Renown pays for them. These are decorative pieces without skill or stat effects.

Hall pieces come as account-bound deeds and can be placed only in a house you own. Wall trophies need a wall. Chopping a piece returns its bound deed. Display-case shelves and inserts can be dyed separately with a plain dye tub; statues and trophies cannot. The starter shop's tradeable cosmetics and decor have different binding rules from Hall deeds.

## Where its halls are

| League location | What to expect |
|---|---|
| New Haven field office, Trammel | Elara Voss, registration, rank services, the shop, and requisitions. Use the Guild Directory's **Show me the way**. |
| Britain, Castle British grounds (planned) | The decided headquarters location. The hall has not been placed by the cited survey. |

New Haven stays as the local chapter when the headquarters is built. The guilds' own main halls across Britannia are also planned, with their New Haven chapters remaining in place. Full shops, requisitions, top-rank guild trials, and travel-network stops are intended for those halls (planned). A guild's top-rank trial is separate from a League story trial.

## Its place in the story: the broken sword and the loops (planned)

The broken sword is what broke time. The League's planned story follows the recovery of its shards, one through each trial chapter. Each shard reveals more about why Britannia loops, and the last chapter reaches the cause.

The story is designed to span six milestone chapters, from Dull Copper through Celestial. The broader loop design connects it to the Setting, a dungeon whose deeper floors open with later loops, and the epilogue called **The Unbroken**. These are planned story elements, not directions to an available trial entrance.

Differences and contradictions found:

- **Milestones:** P47 calls Blaze rank 11 and Mythril rank 14. P48 reports Blaze at 12 and Mythril at 15. F-24 also says "every third rank" while naming Blaze and Mythril. This guide gives the shared milestone names without assigning chapter rank numbers.
- **Chapter count:** F-24 names six chapters but also retains a sentence calling them "five trials."
- **Milestone bonuses:** F-19 and the earlier F-24/F-25 decisions award bonuses per rank. F-24's later decision and P48 instead step bonuses at milestones. Both versions remain in the queue.
- **Commission permissions:** the older design quoted by P47 and F-24 uses Steel and Silver citizens, which are absent from the metal ladder. P47 proposes Verite and Blaze replacements; this guide assigns no posting ranks.
- **Renown spending:** P47, P75, and P179 report no spender. P229 and P243 record built shops that spend it. F-13 still retains the older no-spending description.
- **AP totals and titles:** P47 and F-13 use a 2,505 AP total; P229 reports 1,955 after retired achievements. P229's file summary calls its title set nine, while its picker description and three bought plus five AP titles describe eight. This guide states neither an AP ceiling nor a total title count.
- **Requisitions and Leads:** F-47 and F-48 still present them as future build work; P150 and P203 record them built. F-47's daily refresh has an exception in P150 for live Leads. F-48's doubled drop chance is implemented in P150 as an extra roll with a rising guarantee, and its proposed loot-table qualification becomes an area, creature-group, and Fame check.
- **Non-member standing:** F-24 decides to bank League job rewards until the Apprentice task. P150 records immediate standing for requisitions, including non-members. The planned-job rule and recorded requisition behavior are described separately above.
- **Utility rewards:** P75 proposes a Warehouse and Featherweight satchels; P179 recommends holding or dropping them because they overlap other systems. P75 also proposes dropping the auto-loot Scavenger from F-33. These are proposals, not settled replacements, and are not listed as available rewards.
