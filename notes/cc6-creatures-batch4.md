**The subclass-of-a-ported-creature rule**, written down because `SerpentsFangHighExecutioner : SerpentsFangAssassin`
(batch 2, `[SerializationGenerator(0, false)]`) is the first time this project has subclassed one of its own ports.
Read off the generator's own output rather than inferred: UOContent was compiled in the builder with
`-p:EmitCompilerGeneratedFiles=true` and the emitted `Server.Mobiles.Saliva.Serialization.g.cs`,
`Harpy.Serialization.g.cs` and `SerpentsFangAssassin.Serialization.g.cs` read side by side (stock `Saliva : Harpy` is the
same shape). **Every level that carries the attribute gets three things of its own:** a `(Serial serial) : base(serial)`
constructor, a `private const int SerializationVersion`, and a `Serialize` that calls `base.Serialize(writer)` first and
then writes its own version and fields (`Deserialize` mirrors it). So:

- the child carries its own `[SerializationGenerator(0, false)]` and is `partial`, exactly as if its parent were stock;
- its version starts at 0 and is **independent of the parent's**: the stream is parent-version, parent-fields,
  child-version, child-fields, and a later field on the child is a child-only bump, an ordinary migration;
- a subclass **without** the attribute would have no `(Serial)` constructor — the generator supplies it — and no
  version slot, so it is not an option even for a child that adds nothing.

The test pins the attribute on the child type with `inherit: false` and that the `(Serial)` constructor's
`DeclaringType` is the child. The three keys under `PeerlessKey` follow the same rule.

**Two more generator facts, both learned red (§6):** a `[SerializableField]` always generates a public property named
after its field, `[SerializedCommandProperty]` or not, so a hand-written `WaterLevel` collided (build A); and for a
`[SerializableProperty]` the generator declares the backing field itself (batch 3's Yeast fact, followed here for
`KeyMap`, `TimeLeft`, `TotalIncubationTime`, `Incubating`).

**Two test-host gaps, both closed where S8, batch 1 and batch 2 closed theirs — the fixture patch:**

  server and never by the fixture, so `Kill()` handed `OnDeath(Container)` a null and every ported `c.DropItem` would
  throw. One line, `Server.Items.Corpse.Initialize();`. This batch checks drops through real kills and corpses.
- `BaseCreature.IsBondable` reads `BondingEnabled`, set only by `BaseCreature.Configure()` from `taming.enableBonding`

## 4. Members ported with the creatures, and two decisions

- **`PeerlessKey`, the inert key base (P9, Q-051).** `Item` + blessed + a per-key facet line + a `Lifespan` counted
  down in 10-second slices into a `TimeLeft` property line, ending in `Decay()`: "The ~1_name~ expired...", smoke, sound
  0x201, `Delete()`. ServUO has 20 `: PeerlessKey` descendants; three are here now, all with their final parent the
  first time. **No altar consumes any of them and nothing instances; that is what a ServUO player holds until they
  reach an altar, so it is a remark and not a D-number.** B14 stays WON'T.
- **The subclass rule** (§2), inherited by every later subclass of a ported creature.
- **The persisted cooldown: `ChickenLizard.NextEgg` is a `[SerializableField]`**, absolute `DateTime`, GM-readable,
  following batch 2's `GatheredFur` call and for the same reasons: ServUO persists it (version 1, one `DateTime`); it
  gates a per-instance yield (one egg roll a day after bonding, then one a week); and left volatile every restart would
  re-arm a bonded lizard's roll, which is a deviation nobody would think to look up. Absolute rather than a delta
  because ServUO writes it absolute and the semantics are calendar-shaped ("a week from the last lay"), not
  downtime-shaped. The test pins the attribute on the field.
- **The egg's six members** are persisted in ServUO's order: `IncubationStart`, `TotalIncubationTime`, `Incubating`,
  `Stage`, `WaterLevel`, `IsBattleChicken`; the two whose setters do work are `[SerializableProperty]`.
- **Not ported:** `Incubator` (158 lines, `Container, ISecurable`, all its dependencies stock in pinned, obtained only
  through a `DefCarpentry.cs:229` craft line) and the `Beverage.cs` egg branch. Both are one small task (Q-053); D-69
  and D-70 hold the place.
